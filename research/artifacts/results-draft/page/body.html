SilverTorch is faster than LiNR until you ask for the recall LiNR gives for free

Two families of filtered GPU retrieval on one A100: SilverTorch (Meta's official code, adapted by us) probes a few clusters of an int8 index; LiNR (V1 mask-then-score, V2 gather-then-score, our implementation from the paper) scans every item exactly. This page walks through where each one wins and why.

⚠Provisional, not citable. SilverTorch latencies are bridged from the adapted code's gate grid (its time ÷ our Triton SilverTorch's, applied to the campaign's Triton time) until the full rerun lands. LiNR numbers wait on the final-pass gate. LAION 1–30 M has no adapted-SilverTorch numbers yet.
1 · The production setting

At 24 probes, SilverTorch is 1.5–49× faster, but it returns a different answer

How to read it: each row is a real filter. Left: how many times faster SilverTorch is than the best exact LiNR variant, at one query (hollow) and at a batch of 16 (filled). Right: the share of the true top-100 SilverTorch finds. The speed-up grows with N and batch size; the recall falls with N and with how selective the filter is. On PubMed's five-clause filter it is 49× faster and finds 39 % of the right items.

2 · The price of recall

More probes buy recall, and at a batch of 16 they eat most of the lead

How to read it: synthetic filters with a controlled pass rate. Darker blue means more probes and higher recall (range at the line's end). LiNR's lines barely move with pass rate except V2 at high pass rates. At bs 16 on arXiv-synth, SilverTorch's 0.95-recall setting (512 probes) keeps only a 7 % lead over LiNR V1 when every item passes. At pass rate 0.001 no gated probe count reaches 0.95 at all.

3 · Matched recall

At equal recall, SilverTorch wins most filters up to 3 M items, and LiNR wins wherever SilverTorch cannot get there

How to read it: each cell is the faster family at the chosen recall target. A number is SilverTorch's time as a fraction of LiNR's (0.30 = 3.3× faster). Hatched cells are LiNR wins by default: SilverTorch never reached the target at the probe counts gated so far (up to a quarter of the clusters). Very selective filters, k 1000 and the 10 M sets fall there. More clusters and probes reach 0.95 on PubMed in our Triton records, so those cells are open, not settled.

4 · Inside LiNR

LiNR's V2 beats V1 only for one query at a time

How to read it: below the line V2 (gather the passing items, then score) is faster than V1 (score everything, mask). The paper's claim holds cleanly at bs 1: V2 wins below a pass rate of about 0.8–0.97, and by more as N grows (5× at 30 M). At bs 16 the crossover drops to about 0.02 and V2's edge is at most 13–25 %. Darker = larger index, all at d 256 except Goodreads.

5 · Co-design

Meta's "probe, then filter" pays off only at 10–30 M items

How to read it: above 1, filtering inside the probed clusters (the paper's co-design) beats building the filter over the whole index first. With Meta's code as published, the paper's 1.79–2.15× shows up at 30 M (and more at bs 64), but below 10 M the full-index path is faster. The adapted code is roughly neutral at bs 1 and gains 1.1–1.6× at bs 64 (median), so the claim is about scale, not about the kernel.
