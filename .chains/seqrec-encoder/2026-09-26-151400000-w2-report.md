**Verdict: train PASS; val/test FAIL row for row, PASS as a multiset of rows.**

Mechanism: `yambda.cmd_prep` builds val/test through streaming polars joins on `uid` with no
`maintain_order` and no sort, then drops `uid`. The output row order is therefore a new random
draw on every run. The old `trainer/test.parquet` and the Hub `test.parquet` show the same
effect against each other: same rows, 1 of 45,932 in the same position.

Consequences:
- Row contents are identical.
- Row order is not reproducible, so nothing may be aligned to val/test rows by position.

Not fixed: it is out of scope, and a sort would still not reproduce the old order.

## G-goodreads

**`item_id_map.json`: PASS.** `/data/goodreads-work-id/trainer/item_id_map.json` is sha256-equal
to the Hub copy `/data/goodreads-work-id/item_id_map.json`:
`dd6b80050487971084c398d6d8e8dc07e3f3b9f2b93cacbf68eb7a1148107265`, 797,084 items.

**`test.parquet` against the Hub `/data/goodreads-work-id/test.parquet`: FAIL bit-exact; every
difference is a timestamp tie.**

What was compared:
- Hub schema: `item_ids: large_list<int64>`, `targets: large_list<int64>`, 313,178 rows, no metadata.
- Compared the `item_ids` and `targets` columns: row by row by position, as a multiset of rows, and per differing row.
- Row counts are equal (313,178) and the user order is the same.

Results:
- 274,254 rows are identical.
- 38,924 rows differ:
  - 36,576 differ only in `item_ids` order inside runs of equal timestamps.
  - 782 differ in which items fill the oldest end of the 200-item window. In every one, the length is equal and the difference lies inside the window's leading run of equal timestamps.
  - 2,398 differ only in `targets` order (same multiset).
  - Nothing else differs.
- Column sha256 (new vs Hub): `item_ids` `0f6e94f6…202c` vs `36fb2900…e243`; `targets` `caf0b3b1…e5ad` vs `30adf993…6c60`.
- Full numbers are in `gates_goodreads.json` (`positional` and `pairing` blocks).

Mechanism: `goodreads.cmd_prep` orders events with `sort(["user_id", "ts"])` and nothing breaks
ties. Goodreads has many same-second events (bulk shelving; for example the timestamp minimum
1167638400 is 2007-01-01 00:00). The order inside a tie comes from the multithreaded scan and
join, so it changes run to run. `list.tail(max_seq + 1)` on the full sequence then keeps a
different subset whenever the cut lands in a tie.

Not fixed: it is out of scope, and a tiebreak would also move the output away from the Hub copy.
